Sorting what you found

Is this a Copilot task?

Take each line from your table and ask where it belongs. The question is not which technology is most capable — it is which one already fits the work.

Start here

Microsoft 365 Copilot

The work is reading, writing and summarising

  • Does a person do this in Outlook, Word, Excel, PowerPoint or Teams today?
  • Is the content already in Microsoft 365?
  • Does one person stay in charge of the outcome?

Looks like: drafting a recurring report, summarising a long thread, preparing for a committee, turning notes into minutes.

Your own content grounds the answer. One step, one person, nothing to build.

Second

Copilot Studio

Many people ask the same thing, or a system must be reached

  • Is the audience a whole division, the Directorate, or customers?
  • Does it need a system beyond Microsoft 365 — or multi-step logic and approvals?
  • Does it need its own lifecycle, controls and publishing?

Looks like: a shared assistant answering policy questions for a division, or triaging incoming requests into the right queue.

An agent reaches other systems through connectors, branches on a rule, and can pause for approval.

Third

Power Automate

The steps never vary and no judgement is needed

  • Can the rule be written as “when this happens, always do that”?
  • Must the same input always produce the same output?
  • Is it moving, routing or notifying rather than composing?

Looks like: routing a submitted form for approval, filing attachments to a library, sending a scheduled reminder.

A fixed sequence that runs the same way every time — reliable because it never decides anything.

From your table to the shortlist

Translating a task into a business use case.

A task describes what you do. A business use case says what it costs the bank, what Copilot does about it, and what improves as a result. Four steps, in this order.

1

The task

What you actually do, in your own words — straight from your table.

Read the agenda, previous minutes and background papers before recurring meetings.

2

The business challenge

Why it matters to the unit, with the cost attached. This is the line a Group Head recognises.

Meeting preparation consumes 25 minutes per meeting, 4–5 times a week, across the division.

3

The Copilot use case

What Copilot is asked to do — phrased as an action on named material, not a product feature.

Summarise the invite, the last meeting and the background papers into a briefing before each meeting.

4

The business value

What improves, stated so that someone could tell whether it happened.

Officers arrive prepared, and preparation time falls from 25 minutes to a few.

Check it

A use case is ready when it names a task, not a feeling; carries a frequency and a time cost; says what Copilot does rather than what it is; and states an outcome you could observe. If any of the four is missing, it cannot be scored or given an owner.

The four columns above are the same four your table already holds — which is why the next slide sets every example out in exactly this shape.
